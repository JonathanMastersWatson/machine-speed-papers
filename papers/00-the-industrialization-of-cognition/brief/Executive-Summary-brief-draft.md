## Micropayments never worked

Consumer micropayment systems repeatedly failed: Millicent in the late 1990s, CyberCoin bankrupt in 2001, Peppercoin fading by the mid-2000s. The diagnosis that killed them was correct. Shirky, borrowing Szabo's "mental transaction costs," argued the decision cost of buying exceeds the payment; Odlyzko argued the obstacles were economic, sociological and psychological rather than technological — consumers want predictable, simple pricing. The failure was never the payment. It was the prompt: a human being asked to decide, weigh, and approve, dozens of times a day, over amounts too small to think about.

But metered per-unit pricing has worked continuously at enormous scale wherever the decision was automated. Electricity, mobile data, cloud compute, API calls, per-token inference. Nobody calls those micropayments. The paper's move is an explicit refutation of transferability: the diagnosis that killed micropayments was correct, and it does not apply to agents — because agents do not have mental transaction costs.

## The seller's problem

Humans still appear as sellers, and that threatens the whole argument: if a curator must price, monitor and approve each use, cognitive cost returns on the supply side and everything collapses.

The answer is the same move as the agent's mandate. The seller sets a policy once — who may invoke, for what purpose, at what price, under what terms — and metering runs underneath. The human makes one decision about a class of transactions, not a decision per transaction. This objection is obvious and a reviewer will raise it; the paper answers it in the first ten pages.

## The knowledge stream

Curation becomes an asset. A stream is a mandate with content: what it contains, who may invoke it, at what price, with what attribution — metered input into another stream or model. The economic unit is not the document and not the knowledge. It is the invocation that produced a decision. Grounded contribution earns more than thinking that was retrieved and ignored: evidence is what gets priced, made mechanical.

What makes it licensable: three conditions. The invocation is determined before it commits, witnessed by a disinterested party, and version-bound. Without all three, a stream is publishing, not licensing, and the value accrues to whoever copies it.

## São Paulo

The crop insurer, nineteen hours, 640,000 invocations, 31,000 euros total. The actuary in Toronto earns 2,400 from an eleven-year-old method and finds out on Thursday. Determination at each model holder's own boundary — 640,000 determinations in 640,000 places, each protecting a different owner's asset. This is the paper's thesis in one illustration: judgment, metered per use, settling to the people whose judgment it was.

## Employment unbundled

The handbook assumed buying time bought all of it. That assumption was never tested, because output was unmeasurable and time was the only proxy. Then a credit bureau's employment-verification database — payroll records on over a hundred million American workers, held by a company with no relationship to any of them — was used to scan a thousand staff for undisclosed secondary employment, and two dozen were fired. Two points survive the noise. The only existing cross-firm work record is held by a party with no relationship to the worker. And the fact that mattered was invisible from inside any single employer — disjointness forced not by falsification risk, but because the fact only exists at a vantage point no participant occupies.

## The transitional class

Hyper-productive knowledge workers are already operating as multi-client service providers while contracted as exclusive employees. The form has not caught up to the substance. These are the first to be paid for witnessed output rather than presence — precisely because presence was never what the employer was buying.

## The interpretive class

Position versus judgment. Those whose economic standing rests on being the required intermediary between knowledge and a decision. The consultancy at a third its size lost assembly, not analysis. The actuary did better than the consultancy: he held judgment; they held position. Algorithmic automation targets credentialed knowledge professionals, not only manual workers — and what survives is the judgment, not the seat it was sitting in.

## Aggregate gain, local devastation

Historically compatible, and the actual result. Engels' Pause: aggregate output rising for decades while workers' living standards stagnated. The hand-loom weavers were not retrained — the decline took fifty years; their children entered a different economy. No quantification, no forecasts in this paper — mechanism and historical shape only. The paper refuses the comforting version and the catastrophic version alike: the gains are real, the devastation is local, and both are the same process.

## What this paper cannot answer

The professions were an accountability technology, workable because judgment was inseparable from a person — licence the person, sue the person, strike them off. Separation ends that. What replaces it, when the judgment can be rented without the person, is the question this paper leaves open — and hands to Paper Zero without pre-empting it.
