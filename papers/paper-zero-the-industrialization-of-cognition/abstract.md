# Abstract — The Industrialization of Cognition

Organisations with high AI adoption are producing roughly twice the code of low adopters. They are completing about 27% more work. Hyundai cut digital-twin construction from four weeks to ten days and still cannot justify rolling it out globally, because integration across mismatched robot and PLC vendors doubles the engineering it saved. Insilico reaches a drug candidate in nine months against a traditional baseline near four and a half years, and then waits on clinical trials exactly as long as everyone else. Four industries, four instruments, one measurement. Generation is accelerating faster than verified execution. This paper argues that the preceding papers in this series were not six solutions to six problems. They were the instrumentation of a single transition. Cognition is being industrialized, in the precise sense the word carries: inputs standardised enough to be interchangeable, quality established by gate rather than by inspection after the fact, parts that fit without fitting, and a unit of measure the whole economy can price in. Three of those four conditions are arriving now. The fourth is missing, and its absence is why the productivity gain keeps stalling one layer short of the accounts. The paper proposes the missing unit — the Verified Knowledge Object — and traces what follows for the firm, for the individual, and for the household. It argues the transition is structural rather than inevitable, and names the conditions under which it does not happen. This document is a placeholder. It carries the shape of the argument and some of what that argument is currently running into. It is published early because the useful objections arrive before a paper is finished, not after.
