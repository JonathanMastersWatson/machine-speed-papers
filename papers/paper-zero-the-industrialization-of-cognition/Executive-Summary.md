## A world completely transformed

This paper describes a world completely transformed. Not a world where the same firms, jobs, and markets run faster. A world where what a firm is, what a person sells, how an economy is measured, and who owns the record have all changed — because cognition itself has been industrialized.

It is the same kind of transformation as contract law for the wool merchants, Magna Carta, and the double-entry ledger. Those were not faster ways of doing the same trade. They were new ground rules — what a promise meant across distance, what power was subject to, what an account could prove — and whole economies reorganised around them. The industrialisation of cognition is that kind of change: new ground rules for what cognitive work is, who owns it, how it is measured, and what counts as proof.

## The measurement: it has already started

Organisations with high AI adoption are producing roughly twice the code of low adopters. They are completing about 27% more work. Hyundai cut digital-twin construction from four weeks to ten days and still cannot justify rolling it out globally, because integration across mismatched vendors doubles the engineering it saved. A drug-discovery firm reaches a candidate in nine months against a traditional baseline near four and a half years, and then waits on clinical trials exactly as long as everyone else.

Four industries, four instruments, one measurement. Generation is accelerating faster than verified execution. The gain keeps stalling one layer short of the accounts — and this paper argues that stall is the signature of a transition half-finished, not of a tool overhyped.

## The factory, and where the analogy breaks

This has happened before. Electricity arrived in the 1890s and factory owners wheeled out the steam engine, dropped in a central electric motor, and turned the same overhead driveshaft. Output barely moved. The gain came thirty years later, when engineers stopped treating electricity as bulk power and recognised it as something distributable by wire. The engine was never the constraint. The building was.

The analogy breaks in a way that matters more than the parallel. Electrification took forty years because the constraint was masonry and capital stock. The constraint now decomposes. Part of it is organisational habit — review queues, approval cycles, processes that did not change when the work underneath them collapsed — and that compresses quickly, because the first competitor to remove it leaves the rest no choice. Part of it is not habit at all: incompatible installed equipment, certification, physical validation, biology. That part does not compress.

As the first part falls toward zero, the second becomes the entire binding constraint. Verification stops being one bottleneck among several and becomes the only one. And a verification layer that keeps pace with machine-speed generation cannot be a human review queue — not because humans are slow, but because there is no interval left to stand in.

## The firm, transformed

An org chart is a latency diagram. Every box exists because somebody had to look at something before it could proceed. Spans of control were set by how many people one person could actually review. Layers exist because the reviewer needed a reviewer. Remove the interval and a structure built to hold it has boxes with nothing in them.

The judgment does not disappear. It moves upstream, exercised by fewer people, earlier, with their names on it. It is not the people who run the queue well — the queue is going away. It is the person everyone walks over to ask: the one who knows which exception applies, why the rule reads the way it does, and which part of it nobody has enforced since 2019. They have been there eleven years. They are a grade below the people in the meeting.

Every previous automation wave displaced them first. This one cannot. They are the input. If that reading holds, the first executive decision in this transition is a promotion rather than a restructure — and the window on it closes when a particular cohort retires. The paper is still testing whether it holds.

The transformation reaches further than the org chart. An audit today is a reconstruction, performed months afterwards, on a sample, because a sample is what a human reviewer can afford. When the determination is made at the gate and recorded as it happens, the audit stops being an exercise and becomes a read. Run that across thousands of models and millions of firms and the aggregate changes character: economic data is currently collected, estimated, lagged and revised, a quarter understood two quarters after it ends. A population of verified determinations arrives as it occurs. The measurement stops being a survey of the economy and starts being a byproduct of it.

## What becomes possible when the conditions arrive

Watch each condition arrive for cognition. A trade archive is forty years of knowledge no agent can use, because it is packaged for a human reader. Converted once — each assertion bound to an exact span in a named source, hashed, licence travelling with the object — knowledge stops being a document and becomes an input. A claims assessor's judgment, made once in advance with her name on it instead of ninety seconds at a time with no record, makes quality a gate rather than a reconstruction. A credential issued in Lagos resolves in under a second for a stranger in Rotterdam, because the questions that matter — was the assessor authorised, was the rubric current, could anyone have altered the record — are checkable without trust.

And this is where it becomes an economy. A drone buys insurance for one eleven-minute flight for nine cents. Not a fleet policy — this flight, this route, this airframe's witnessed maintenance history. The product was never too dangerous to sell. It was too small, because underwriting it cost three orders of magnitude more than the premium. A publisher licenses a corpus per retrieval and learns for the first time what any single piece of its knowledge is worth.

## The missing unit

One condition is still missing, and the paper is honest about it. There is no unit. Electricity got the kilowatt-hour. Shipping got the twenty-foot equivalent. We meter tokens. Tokens are coal — fuel going in, not work coming out. Nobody can yet buy a verified decision, which means nobody can price one, which is why the gain keeps stalling one layer short of the accounts. The metrology gap and the productivity gap are the same gap.

The paper proposes the unit. A Verified Knowledge Object — a VKO — is a single piece of cognitive work that can be bought, because it carries the proof of its own admissibility. Cognition was applied, a gate admitted it, something with no stake recorded that this happened, and an obligation came into existence. A token measures effort expended. A VKO measures work completed. Firms will report revenue per VKO the way they once reported revenue per employee.

## The individual, transformed

The same mechanism arrives for the individual as a change in what a person sells. The assessor's judgment was consumed one case at a time for twenty-two years. Compiled, it is made once and applied to every case that follows. A wage is linear in time. A royalty is linear in use. That conversion was available to authors, composers and inventors, and to almost nobody else. It is now available to anyone whose judgment can be rendered precisely enough to execute.

So the person's economic identity stops being a title and becomes a history. Not what they claim to know. What they have been right about, at what rate, in public, over how long. Anyone can rent the same model. Nobody else holds fourteen thousand witnessed determinations in a narrow domain, each one checkable by a stranger. The scarce thing was never the cognition. It is the provenance on it.

## The household, transformed

A personal agent levels the field. Everything above is a firm getting better instruments. This is the individual getting the same ones. On one side of every lease, premium and fare is a system that has priced ten million comparable cases; on the other is a person with an afternoon. That asymmetry is recent, and it exists because the cost of running a pricing agent fell for institutions before it fell for everyone else. It is now falling for everyone else. An agent that holds a mandate, broadcasts the terms its principal will accept, and negotiates at the speed the counterparty is already operating at does not give the individual an advantage. It restores the symmetry that existed when both parties to a deal were a person.

## Who owns the record

A system that records everything is a surveillance apparatus if one party owns the recording. The witness is built from parts that have existed for a decade — a hash chain, a timestamp authority, storage under separate credentials, a published verifier — and it does exactly one job. It listens. It cannot permit, deny, delay or flag, and anything that does any of those is not a witness but an unacknowledged second gate. Because it is trivial to build, it cannot be cornered. Because the specification is free, there is no toll worth routing around. What stays unowned is the record.

## What remains unresolved

The paper argues this transition is structural rather than inevitable, and names the conditions under which it does not happen. Several things are unresolved and stated as such: who owns compiled judgment when the contract assigning it was written for labour consumed on use; whether provenance becomes heritable; whether the measurement gap closes by agreement or by someone with pricing power requiring it.

The full paper will carry a hundred-year extrapolation, written twice — once hopeful and once not — because the fork is real and it is decided inside the next decade.
